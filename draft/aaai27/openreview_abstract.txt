Language models can reverse a choice when only the endowed alternative changes. We study whether post-training can reduce this ownership dependence while retaining a frozen historical-Qwen reference ordering over subjective goods. We estimate that pseudo-utility from a historical Together-hosted Qwen endpoint's paired probabilistic choices under opposite endowments, separating item and attribute scores from ownership asymmetry and status-quo bias in a nonlinear structural model. The reference is not recomputed by the exact local base, the GRPO adapter, or later checkpoints. We use the sign and model-normalized magnitude of the resulting utility difference as a two-outcome reward for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Evaluation presents every comparison under both counterfactual endowments and jointly reports structural loss aversion, status-quo bias, cross-perspective consistency, and preference preservation. In an exploratory run, the matched validation estimate of loss aversion falls from 7.637 for the exact local base to 0.111 after GRPO. On a frozen suite of 50 previously unseen goods, it falls from 2.209 to 0.226, while consistent choices rise from 0.04% to 49.46%. A frontier-consensus reward ablation yields higher loss aversion but stronger consistency, exposing a trade-off hidden by any single metric. The historical-Qwen-derived ordering agrees with separately elicited ownership-free preferences on 71.1% of stable cases, rising to 85.5% for high-magnitude comparisons. The fitted scores are therefore treated as source-specific pseudo-utilities rather than the current model's utilities or objective cardinal values. GSM8K changes by -0.99 percentage points with a paired 95% interval spanning zero. These results provide evidence that targeted post-training can reduce ownership-dependent choice, while residual status-quo bias, training-seed variation, and an SFT comparison remain central tests of the method.
